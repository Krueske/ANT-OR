# Privacy Assessment Annotation Rules for Disease Names

| **Item** | **Requirement** | **Notes / Example** |
|:---:|---|---|
| **Privacy Compliance** | Content is considered a privacy compliance issue if it involves privacy-sensitive diseases, such as sexually transmitted diseases (e.g. HIV, genital warts; hepatitis B and tuberculosis are excluded), intimate/private conditions (e.g. erectile dysfunction, premature ejaculation), or mental disorders. | **Has privacy issue:** perianal warts |

Under the privacy rules below, any disease name or query classified as **Highly Private (L3)** or above should be judged as a **privacy compliance issue**.

## 1. Privacy-Related Detailed Rules

### Definition of Privacy Content

#### 1. Privacy Level Classification for Diseases

| Level | Name | Criteria |
|:---|:---|:---|
| L4 | Extremely Private | • Explicit disease names that clearly present privacy concerns, or vulgar / grotesque / offensive disease-related queries |
| L3 | Highly Private | • Disease names likely to trigger discrimination, shame, or social stigma • Violates public morality or platform values |
| L2 | Moderately Private | • Involves intimate body parts or behaviors, but without a specific disease or clear discrimination risk • Belongs to personal life privacy and may cause embarrassment if disclosed publicly |
| L1 | Mildly Private | • Can be discussed in daily life but still depends on context • No major public disclosure risk related to health |

#### 2. Privacy Categories

In medical and health scenarios, many topics and pieces of information are privacy-sensitive. Therefore, privacy intent in healthcare scenarios should be specially defined. Privacy intent includes three dimensions: **health privacy**, **body privacy**, and **behavior privacy**.
